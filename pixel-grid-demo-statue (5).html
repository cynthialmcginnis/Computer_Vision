What This Means

When you show a machine a photograph, it does not "see" the photograph. It sees a massive array of numbers. For this statue image, that is 36,578,304 numbers. Each number is between 0 and 255.

The machine's task is to find the pattern in those numbers. Is this a face? Is that moss? Is that stone? The pattern that makes a statue a statue is encoded somewhere in the arrangement of 36,578,304 numbers. The machine must learn to recognize that pattern.

This is the fundamental challenge of computer vision. How do you extract meaning from such vast numerical arrays?
Key Insight: When you look at a photograph, you see a unified image with meaning. When a machine looks at the same photograph, it sees 36,578,304 numbers. The difference between human vision and machine vision is the difference between image and number. Teaching a machine to "see" is teaching it to extract meaning from numbers.
